# Permits research — raw findings (checked September 2026)

Format: jurisdiction -> finding -> URL -> date checked. Compiled for `site/content/c_permits.py`.
Research budget used: web search + fetch, primary sources preferred (Municode/city/county sites, permit portals, property appraiser sites). Municode's JS-rendered pages and two `elaws.us` mirrors repeatedly timed out or refused to render for direct fetch during this session; where that happened, findings rely on search-engine snippets of those same pages or on a compiled comparison document, and that limitation is noted.

## State law verification (before writing anything)

- HB 683 (2025) / Chapter 2025-140, Laws of Florida — the Florida Senate's own "Citations - Statutes" list for HB 683 shows exactly one statute created for synthetic turf: **125.572**. No companion municipal section (e.g., under Chapter 166) was created. -> https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2025/683 (Citations - Statutes tab) -> checked 2026-09-21.
- Text of F.S. 125.572(3) uses the term "local government," not "county," in its preemption clause, even though the section is codified in Chapter 125 (County Government). A statewide news outlet's coverage of the bill is headlined "Cities losing authority to regulate artificial turf lawns," confirming municipalities read the statute as covering them too. -> https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/0125.572 and https://amisun.com/cities-losing-authority-to-regulate-artificial-turf-lawns/ -> checked 2026-09-21.
- Conclusion used on every page: cite only F.S. 125.572 and "Chapter 2025-140, Laws of Florida," and describe it as covering both counties and cities by its own "local government" wording. Do not invent a separate municipal statute number.

### City of Orlando
- Finding: Unlike the other seven jurisdictions, Orlando does appear to regulate artificial turf specifically. Search results describe a city ordinance that "amends Chapter 60, Part 2, and Chapter 66 of the Land Development Code to provide specific regulations for artificial turf and standards for the installation and maintenance of artificial turf" (Chapter 60 = Subdivision and Landscaping; Part 2 = Landscaping and Tree Protection; Chapter 66 = Definitions). -> https://library.municode.com/fl/orlando/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TITIICICO_CH60SULA -> checked 2026-09-21. Direct fetches of Municode and of an `elaws.us` mirror both failed (timeout / connection reset) during this session, so the specific requirements below come from a secondary source, not Orlando's code text directly.
- Secondary source detail: a public-records comparison of Florida cities' artificial-turf ordinances, compiled by the City of Belle Isle, FL (a neighboring Orange County city, not one of our eight pages) and posted on Belle Isle's own site, summarizes Orlando's rule as: turf classed as impervious because the landscape code only treats "living" material as pervious; material must look natural in color (other colors need Appearance Review Officer sign-off); installed outside any tree's drip line; standard manufacturer-spec installation, anchoring and seam rules; barred within 50 feet of any water body, within drainage features, within Historic Preservation Districts, and within public or private right-of-way; indoor/outdoor plastic or nylon carpeting can't be used as turf; allowed on residential lots, private parks, schools, commercial sites and limited park play areas; requires an Engineering Permit with a signed/sealed survey, turf placement and dimensions, impervious-surface-ratio (ISR) calculations, product specs and an erosion-control statement. -> https://www.belleislefl.gov/sites/default/files/fileattachments/community/page/9922/artificial_turf_comparisons_of_local_florida_ordinances_-_sheet1.pdf -> checked 2026-09-21. No date is printed on the document and it does not mention DEP Rule 62-308.100, so it may predate the May 2026 state rule; whether Orlando has since amended these sections could not be confirmed and should be checked directly with Permitting Services before a job is designed around it.
- Permitting: Orlando Permitting Services Division, 400 S. Orange Ave. Phone (407) 246-2121. Online portals: Orlando Permitting Portal (digitalpermits.orlando.gov) and Permit Lookup (permitlookup.cityoforlando.net/WebPermits). -> https://www.orlando.gov/Our-Government/Departments-Offices/Economic-Development/Permitting-Services -> checked 2026-09-21.
- Water: Orlando Utilities Commission (OUC) is the electric/water utility for most of the city; OUC sets its own irrigation schedule separate from Orange County Utilities. Could not confirm OUC's current specific watering days/times within this session's research budget; the page tells readers to check their OUC bill or ouc.com. -> https://www.ouc.com/about/water-services/ -> checked 2026-09-21.
- Property appraiser: Orange County Property Appraiser (Orlando is in Orange County). -> https://ocpafl.org/ -> checked 2026-09-21.

## Things flagged as unverifiable / left out or hedged

- No jurisdiction among the eight published a statement, post-May 19, 2026, saying its office had changed its practice because of Rule 62-308.100. The pages say what the code says and note the preemption, without claiming a change in office practice.
- Whether a residential turf lawn specifically needs a building/zoning permit in Kissimmee, Osceola, St. Cloud, Orange County, Polk, Lake or Seminole could not be confirmed either way (no turf-specific code section, no FAQ or handout found saying yes or no). Each of those seven pages says this directly and tells the homeowner to call before starting, rather than guessing "no permit needed."
- Orlando's ordinance detail is sourced from a secondary comparison document (Belle Isle, FL), not Orlando's own code text, because Municode and an elaws.us mirror both failed to render for direct fetch in this session. The page says so and recommends confirming current requirements with Orlando Permitting Services.
- OUC's specific irrigation-day schedule for Orlando customers could not be confirmed in this session; the page points to OUC's own site/bill instead of stating specific days.
- Whether Orange County's Chapter 24 has been amended since May 2026 in light of the state rule could not be confirmed (the page reflects a search-engine read of the Municode page, not a rendered fetch of the current text).
